Moving to the Beat: Dance Tutorial for Neurodivergent Creators

Hey friends, Maha here — my Bronx heart beats to the rhythm of movement, and today I’m sharing a warm‑up routine that feels like a gentle hug for my neurodivergent brain. When I step into the lab, the hum of the air‑conditioning, the soft click of my laptop keys, and the subtle echo of my own breath become a symphony I can dance with.

First, I close my eyes and let a soft breath in, feeling the air fill my ribs like a low‑frequency bass that grounds me. I then exhale slowly, letting tension dissolve. I repeat this three times, syncing the breath to a slow count‑one‑two‑three‑four.

Next, I stand tall, feet hip‑width apart, and gently sway my hips side‑to‑side, like a pendulum in a quiet studio. I imagine the swing of a sound‑proofed wall—solid, steady, keeping the outside world at bay. This motion awakens my core and reminds me how Edan’s soundproofing tips let me create space for these movements without unwanted noise. (Check out his article on the 4 elements of soundproofing for more on building a calm environment.)

Now, I add arm circles—big, slow circles that trace the arc of a speaker’s cone. As the arms rise, I feel the vibration of melodic frequencies, and as they lower, I release any lingering jittery thoughts.Finally, I finish with a simple step‑touch combo: step right, touch left foot, step left, touch right, each step matching a gentle click‑clack that echoes in my studio’s insulated walls. This pattern grounds my feet, my mind, and my heartbeat.

These moves are short—just five minutes—but they reset my sensory palette, making room for creativity. Try them before you start a new beat or recording session, and notice how the world feels a little softer, a little more in tune with you.
