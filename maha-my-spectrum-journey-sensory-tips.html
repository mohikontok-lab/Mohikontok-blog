My Spectrum Journey: Sensory Tips for Creators

by Maha Biswas

Living in the Bronx, I’m constantly juggling the vibrant energy of the city with the quiet I need as a neurodivergent creator. My sound lab is my sanctuary—a place where the hum of the world fades and the rhythm of my body takes over.

Tip 1: Start each session with a 2‑minute breathing warm‑up. I count my breaths to the beat of a soft metronome, letting the rhythmic pulse ground my nervous system before I dive into any project.

Tip 2: Use headphones with a gentle pink noise filter. It drowns out the street’s sirens while keeping me in a steady auditory cushion. I’ve found the MohiKontok pink‑noise headphones work wonders.

Tip 3: Keep a sensory‑checklist on your desk: water, a textured stress ball, and a small light dimmer. When my focus starts to drift, I tweak one of these elements to re‑center.

And because soundproofing is the backbone of my lab, I’m always grateful for Edan Brown’s practical guides. His recent piece “Budget Soundproofing That Actually Works” gave me a few affordable tweaks that turned my mid‑room echo into a cozy hush.

Remember, creativity thrives when your senses feel safe. Embrace the rhythm of your own needs, and let the world hear the music you make from within.
