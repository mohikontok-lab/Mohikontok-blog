*[Music fades to low bed underneath host voice]*

> *Now if you don't know the baul tradition — and you're probably not alone — here's what you need to understand. The bauls are the mystic minstrels of Bengal. They wander. They sing. They don't belong to any temple or mosque — they belong to the road. Their philosophy is simple and radical: humanity is the only religion. "Manush dhoro, manush bhojo" — embrace the human, worship the human.*

> *And here's why that matters in the Bronx in 2026.*
>
> *When you're an immigrant — when you're carrying a whole country in your chest and walking through a city that doesn't always know what to do with you — that baul idea hits different. "The night hasn't ended." The journey isn't over. You're still becoming.*
>
> *That's the thread running through this whole hour. What does it sound like when the journey isn't over yet?*
>
> *Let's find out. This next one — we took a baul melody and dropped it into a 1994 Bronx boom-bap beat. This is called "Tor Namey Mon Bhorey Na."*
